Campaign Tracking Standards - Education for All 2026

This page translates campaign measurement into plain English. It gives stakeholders a way to create consistent links without needing to understand GA4 internals.

Why UTM parameters matter

UTM parameters tell us where traffic came from, which campaign drove it, and what version of a message performed best. Without consistent naming, channel reporting becomes noisy and hard to trust. With consistent naming, teams can compare paid, owned, partner, and offline activity using the same structure.

Parameter definitions

ParameterPurpose
utm_sourceIdentifies the platform, sender, or referring property driving the visit.
utm_mediumDescribes the traffic type such as paid_social, email, referral, or offline.
utm_campaignGroups links into the high-level campaign initiative.
utm_contentSeparates creative versions, placements, or message variants.
utm_termOptional field for keywords or extra classification when needed.

Naming rules

These rules are intentionally strict so reporting stays clean across teams and agencies.

Use lowercase only

Avoid uppercase letters so the same source is not split into multiple values in reporting.

Use underscores

Separate words with underscores, not spaces or mixed punctuation.

Keep campaign names stable

Use one canonical campaign value such as education_for_all_2026 across all channels.

Describe the medium precisely

Choose values like paid_social, organic_social, email, or offline.

Use content for versioning

Track creative differences such as carousel_v1 or launch_video_link.

Common mistakes

These are the naming issues most likely to break reporting consistency.

Mixing uppercase and lowercase

Facebook and facebook will split the same source into two values.

Changing campaign names mid-flight

If one team uses education_for_all_2026 and another uses edu_for_all, rollup reporting gets muddy.

Putting whole sentences in content

UTM values should stay short, repeatable, and readable in reports.
