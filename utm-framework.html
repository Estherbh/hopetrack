Why it matters

Why UTM parameters matter

UTM parameters make it possible to understand exactly where campaign traffic came from, how it was delivered, and which message or creative drove action. Without a shared naming structure, channel performance becomes harder to compare, attribution becomes messy, and optimization decisions lose credibility. A clean UTM framework gives both technical and non-technical stakeholders a common language for campaign measurement.

Rules

Naming Convention Rules

Rule 1

Always lowercase - use "facebook" not "Facebook".

Rule 2

Use underscores not spaces - use "paid_social" not "paid social".

Rule 3

Be specific on utm_campaign - include the year.

Rule 4

Use utm_content to identify the creative version.

Rule 5

Never launch a campaign without UTM parameters.

Warnings

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Do not reuse one UTM across channels.

Never use the same UTM for different channels - you will lose attribution clarity.

Do not skip utm_content.

Always use utm_content to distinguish creatives - otherwise A/B testing is impossible.

Do not mix cases.

UTM parameters are case-sensitive in some tools - always use lowercase.
